How to Use RAM and CPU at Full Power and Speed

To run your CPU and RAM at their maximum advertised speeds and full power capacity, you must unlock performance profiles in your motherboard's BIOS and configure Windows to stop throttling your hardware.

By default, manufacturers cap these speeds out of the box to prioritize power saving and stability. Here is how to unlock their full potential safely.

⚡ Part 1: Unlock Full RAM Speed (BIOS)

When you buy high-speed RAM (e.g., 3200MHz, 6000MHz), it defaults to a much slower baseline speed (like 2133MHz or 4800MHz) until you turn on its performance profile.

  1. Restart your PC and repeatedly press the BIOS key (usually Delete or F2) as it boots up.
  2. Look for a setting named XMP (Intel platforms), EXPO (AMD AM5 platforms), or D.O.C.P. / E.O.C.P. (older AMD platforms).
  3. Change this setting from Disabled or Auto to Profile 1.
  4. Press F10 to save your settings and exit. Your RAM will now run at its true maximum rated speed.

🏎️ Part 2: Unlock Full CPU Power (Windows)

Windows has built-in power-saving modes that artificially slow down your processor when it thinks it doesn’t need it. You can force it to run at 100% capacity at all times.

1. Enable the Hidden "Ultimate Performance" Power Plan

  1. Click the Start menu, type cmd, right-click Command Prompt, and choose Run as administrator.
  2. Copy and paste the following command and press Enter: 
    powercfg -duplicatescheme e9a42b02-d5df-448d-aa00-03f14749eb61
  3. Open your Control Panel and navigate to Power Options.
  4. Select the newly unlocked Ultimate Performance plan.

2. Disable CPU Core Parking

Windows frequently "parks" (shuts down) CPU cores to save power, which introduces tiny stutters when they have to wake back up.

  1. Press Win + R, type regedit, and hit Enter.
  2. Go to Edit > Find (or press Ctrl + F), type ValueMin, and search.
  3. Keep searching until you find a key named ValueMin inside a folder path that contains 0cc5b647-c1df-4637-891a-dec35c318583.
  4. Double-click ValueMin and change its Value Data to 0.
  5. Find ValueMax right next to it, and ensure its Value Data is set to 100. Click OK and restart.

🛠️ Direct Comparison: Tweaks vs. Reality

Component Default Behavior Full Power Behavior Trade-offs to Expect
RAM Restricted to safe JEDEC base speeds (e.g. 2133MHz) Operates at full advertised overclock speeds via XMP/EXPO Minor increase in system heat; potential instability if RAM is faulty.
CPU Continuously drops clock speed and parks idle cores Locks clock speed at maximum base frequency; instantaneous response Higher idle electricity usage; PC fans will run louder.

⚠️ A Crucial Warning on Temperatures

Running your hardware at full speed means your PC will generate significantly more heat. You must monitor your temperatures to make sure your components aren't thermal throttling (slowing themselves down to prevent melting):

  • Download a free tool like HWMonitor or HWInfo64.
  • Ensure your CPU stays below 85°C (185°F) under heavy load. If it pushes past 90°C–95°C, your cooling system cannot handle the extra power, and you should revert to standard power settings.
